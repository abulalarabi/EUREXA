# EUREXA: End-User Reconfiguration of Environment with eXplainable Augmentation for Generative Fabrication

This repository is the official project site for our research paper on EUREXA.

## About

Augmentation enables the rapid reconfiguration of passive physical interfaces to improve accessibility, support independent living through domestic automation, and more. However, its potential is largely unrealized for novice users due to several key barriers.
First, users rarely identify latent interaction problems within their built environments. Second, they have little to no knowledge to precisely describe design intent for systems to interpret. Third, the most innovative solutions are often confined to research prototypes, limiting practical access. These barriers make meaningful customization and everyday innovation inaccessible to the end-user.

We introduce EUREXA, an agentic AI system to share the spirit of discovery (“Eureka!”). EUREXA is built for end-users to support a \textit{diagnose–discover–describe} flow: Based on user input that could vary in its ambiguity and complexity, it reveals latent interaction challenges in existing objects that were overlooked, presents rich reconfiguration opportunities through augmentations, and proposes enhancement strategies to adapt to individuals' physical settings. 
Its novelty lies in a dual search space that spans both public repositories of augmentation designs (e.g., Thingiverse) and research articles, enabling the construction of reusable designs even when no libraries or parametric tools exist, from research knowledge as written in the articles.
EUREXA transforms non-parametric models into parametric ones or generates fully explainable designs directly. 
To validate EUREXA's performance with various user inputs with different forms, complexities, and clarity, we developed metrics to evaluate the ambiguity of user input, conducted a user study, and present our findings on what critical factors are to advance generative AI to assist end-users in generative fabrication to readily augment physical interfaces.
